PROJECT PROMPT: Build “Dhaka WasteTrack”

An Interactive Municipal Waste Management & Public Accountability Platform for CPD Climate Week 2026

1. Project Background and Purpose

Build a professional, visually polished, responsive, and interactive website prototype called Dhaka WasteTrack — Municipal Waste Operations & Public Dashboard.

This website is a demonstration prototype for our team, MacroMind, participating in the CPD Climate Week 2026 Climate Policy Case Competition in Bangladesh.

Our case topic is:

“Urban Waste Management in Bangladesh: Towards an Enforceable Municipal Waste Management Plan.”

The purpose of the website is to visually demonstrate how Dhaka and other major cities in Bangladesh could use an integrated digital platform to monitor municipal waste collection, transportation, weighing, treatment, resource recovery, and final disposal.

The website must directly address these two requirements from our case competition:

Requirement C — Digital Platform

“Propose a digital platform linking collection routes, vehicles, transfer stations, weighbridges, treatment facilities, and disposal sites.”

Requirement D — Public Municipal Dashboard

“Recommend a public municipal dashboard showing service performance by ward and contractor.”

The website should make these recommendations tangible. Judges should be able to open the website, click different components, explore the information, change filters, inspect charts, track a demonstration vehicle, and understand how the proposed system would work in practice.

This is a demonstration prototype using clearly labelled synthetic data, not a live government system. Do not claim to have access to actual Dhaka city corporation databases, GPS feeds, official vehicle information, or live municipal records.

2. Primary Objectives

The completed website must demonstrate the following:

1. An integrated digital platform connecting collection routes, waste collection vehicles, transfer stations, weighbridges, recycling and treatment facilities, and final disposal sites.
2. A public dashboard showing waste-management performance by ward and contractor.
3. A transparent method for monitoring collection operations and identifying missed collections.
4. A waste-tracking workflow from the original collection point to the final destination.
5. Measurable key performance indicators (KPIs) for waste management.
6. A complaint-monitoring interface that allows users to explore how service issues could be reported and resolved.
7. A waste-management model that prioritises segregation, recycling, organic waste treatment, and resource recovery before considering Waste-to-Energy (WtE).
8. A visually convincing demonstration suitable for a university-level policy case competition.

The most important principle throughout the website is:

“Reform First. Recover Value. Use WtE Only When Viable.”

Do not present Waste-to-Energy as a substitute for basic waste-management reform.

Design the website primarily for the following audiences:

- Judges and evaluators of the CPD Climate Policy Case Competition.
- City corporation officials and municipal waste-management planners.

Provide an “Indicator Definitions” panel with formulas.

Examples:

Collection Completion Rate

Completed scheduled collection tasks ÷ Total scheduled collection tasks × 100.

On-Time Collection Rate

Collections completed on time ÷ Total scheduled collection tasks × 100.

Missed Collection Rate

Missed scheduled collection tasks ÷ Total scheduled collection tasks × 100.

Complaint Resolution Rate

Resolved complaints ÷ Total complaints received × 100.

Material Recovery Rate

Recovered material weight ÷ Recorded total incoming waste weight × 100.

For the recovery rate, clearly identify the denominator, reporting period, and whether the calculation covers all incoming waste or only eligible waste.

Do not calculate a KPI if the necessary data is missing. Show “Insufficient Data” rather than misleading users.

If a KPI has an illustrative target, label it “Proposed Target” and do not present it as an official statutory requirement.

13. Module Eight: Waste Recovery and Treatment

Create a dedicated page explaining how the digital platform supports different waste-treatment pathways.

Use a visual process diagram.

Source Segregation → Separate Collection → Waste Weighing and Recording → Recovery / Treatment → Safe Residual Disposal

Show the following categories:

A. Organic Waste

Explain that appropriate organic waste can be routed to composting or anaerobic digestion, depending on its quality and the capability of the facility.

Track:

- Quantity received.
- Quantity processed.
- Product or recovered output, where recorded.
- Residual quantity.
- Facility status.

B. Recyclable Waste

Show how paper, plastics, metals, glass, and other suitable materials can be separated and directed to Material Recovery Facilities or other appropriate recovery channels.

Track:

- Quantity collected.
- Quantity sorted.
- Quantity recovered for recycling.
- Residual material.
- Destination record.

C. Hazardous Waste

Provide a separate pathway for suitable hazardous household waste.

Explain the importance of segregated collection, secure temporary storage, safe handling, and appropriate treatment.

Do not route hazardous waste through a normal composting process or an ordinary municipal waste pathway.

D. Residual Waste

Explain that residual waste is the material remaining after appropriate recovery and treatment options have been considered.

Show how it is recorded and directed to an appropriate final destination.

E. Waste-to-Energy

Present WtE as a conditional option.

The interface should explain that major WtE investment should only proceed when its suitability has been demonstrated.

Use four decision gates:

1. Technical: Is there reliable, suitable feedstock in sufficient quantity and quality?
2. Financial: Can the project cover operating costs, debt service, and a reasonable return under the proposed financing structure?
3. Environmental and Social: Are required approvals, emission controls, safe residual management, and worker protections adequately addressed?
4. Governance: Are procurement, contracts, reporting, and independent oversight sufficiently transparent?

Provide two possible outcomes:

- All required gates passed: Proceed to the relevant approval and investment process.
- One or more gates not passed: Defer major WtE expansion and strengthen material recovery, composting, and other suitable treatment options.

Do not label any proposed facility as viable without supporting evidence.
